Physical Camera

Switch the Camera tab's Physical Camera toggle on and the photo camera stops being a FOV slider and becomes a camera: focal length in millimeters on a real sensor, and an exposure triangle — f-stop, shutter speed, ISO — coupled the way a photographer expects. Unity's own physical camera properties drive exposure and depth of field under HDRP only; on URP and Built-in they are inert with respect to the post stack. Photo Mode Pro's model works on all three.

Lens and sensor¶

  • Focal Length (12–200 mm) and the sensor preset (Full Frame, APS-C, Micro 4/3, Super 35, 16 mm) drive the field of view through real lens geometry — 24 mm feels wide, 85 mm compresses a portrait, and the same lens gets tighter on a crop sensor, exactly as it should.
  • While the model is on, the FOV slider hides (the lens owns it); switch the toggle off to return to direct FOV control.

The exposure triangle¶

F-Stop, Shutter and ISO snap to the standard photographic stop sequences (f/0.7–f/16, 1/1000 s–1 s, ISO 100–6400) with a live EV readout. With Lock Exposure on (the default), the triangle is coupled: open the aperture two stops and the shutter compensates two stops the other way — constant exposure, different look. Each leg drives its photographic consequence:

Leg Drives
F-stop Depth-of-field strength — the same public Adjustments.Aperture as always, so nothing breaks
Shutter Motion-blur weight (where the pipeline offers the effect)
ISO Film-grain intensity — base ISO is clean, ISO 6400 is gritty
EV delta Exposure compensation when you unlock the triangle

On pipelines that cannot express a coupling (the Built-in fallback has no depth of field, for example) the control stays visible and functional for the legs it can drive — reported through capabilities, never silently swallowed.

  • Blades shapes the bokeh via the aperture blade count where the pipeline supports it (HDRP); the row hides elsewhere.
  • On HDRP the photo camera itself runs in physical mode (focal length, sensor, aperture, shutter, ISO and blade count on the camera), so HDRP's own physical depth of field and exposure respond too.

Exact restore¶

The physical model only ever touches the photo camera — the clone that exists while photo mode is open. Your game's camera keeps its own properties, untouched, before, during and after; the model's state persists across photo sessions so your lens setup is still there next time.

Scripting¶

var controller = PhotoModeController.Instance;
var physical = controller.PhysicalCamera;
physical.Enabled = true;
physical.CaptureBaseEv();          // lock the current exposure
physical.FocalLengthMm = 85f;
physical.SetAperture(1.4f);        // shutter compensates automatically
controller.NotifyPhysicalCameraEdited();

PhotoModeExposureTriangle exposes the pure math (EV, compensation, stop snapping, lens geometry) if you want the relationships without the model.
