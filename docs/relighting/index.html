Runtime Relighting

The Light tab is a full lighting rig that exists only inside photo mode: add up to six photo lights, orbit them around your subject, tune color and intensity, dim the scene's own lights — and everything is torn down and restored exactly when you leave. No other Unity asset offers runtime light placement as a photo-mode capability; the lighting tools on the store are editor-side preset managers.

Rig lights live under a root object the system creates (PhotoModePro Light Rig), never inside your scene hierarchy, and are destroyed on exit. Scene lights you touch through the rig are snapshotted before the first change and restored field-for-field on exit, under the same contract as time scale, audio and cursor state.

Adding lights¶

The five add buttons create a light pre-configured for a photographic role:

Kind Type Starts as
Key Spot Bright warm-neutral, ahead-left of the subject, 35° above the horizon
Fill Point Soft cool light opposite the key, no shadows
Rim Spot Hard white light behind the subject for edge separation
Practical Point Small warm lantern-style glow near the subject
Sun Directional Scene-wide golden light

Rig presets replace the current rig with a complete setup: Three-Point, Rembrandt, Rim Only, Golden Hour, Moonlight and Practical Warm. Apply one, then edit any light it created.

Placing the selected light¶

Placement is free: put the light anywhere. The primary tool is the mouse — grab the light's on-screen marker and drag it, and it follows the cursor exactly, moving in the camera-parallel plane through the light (turn or move the camera and drag again to move in depth). Clicking a marker selects that light.

The panel shows the position as live, unbounded X / Y / Z world coordinates with step buttons for fine, gamepad-friendly adjustment, plus:

  • Distance — meters from the focus point (the spot photo mode opened at); sliding it moves the light radially toward or away from your subject.

Lights always aim at the focus point, so wherever you put one, it lights your subject. Markers are tinted with their light's color, hide with the rest of the UI (H), and can never appear in a capture.

Follow Camera anchors the orbit to the camera view instead of the world: the whole setup travels with the shot as you re-frame, so a rim stays a rim.

Light properties¶

  • Type cycles Point → Spot → Area → Sun where the active pipeline supports them (realtime area lights exist on HDRP only; the option is hidden elsewhere).
  • Intensity is entered in Unity intensity units everywhere. On HDRP — which lights in physical units — the readout converts to real photometric values: lumens for point, spot and area lights, lux for directional, so the same slider position reads comparably on every pipeline.
  • Hue / Color Sat set the light color (saturation 0 is white).
  • Range, Spot Angle and Shadows behave exactly like their Unity counterparts; controls that do not apply to the current type hide themselves.

Scene Lights¶

The Scene Lights slider is a master dimmer for the scene's own lighting: every light that was already in the scene and the ambient environment (ambient intensity and colors, reflection intensity). Pull it down to darken the game's lighting, then build the shot with rig lights alone.

This slider is the relight mechanism, not a garnish. Physically, a rig light follows the inverse-square law — at typical distances it adds only a few units of illumination, invisible against daylight. Darkening the frame with negative exposure does not help: exposure attenuates the rig's own contribution equally. Dim the scene instead and the rig owns the light; even modest intensities read like studio strobes against a dimmed base.

The dimmer also darkens the sky. Skybox scenes get a session copy of the skybox material with its exposure (or tint) scaled down; skybox-less scenes have their solid-color camera backgrounds scaled instead. One exception: a custom skybox shader that exposes no _Exposure, _Tint, _TintColor or _Color property cannot be dimmed and keeps its authored brightness.

The slider scales realtime lighting only. Baked lights — and the baked half of Mixed lights — live in the lightmaps, which the dimmer cannot touch, so their contribution stays at full brightness (a warning is logged when the scene contains such lights).

Everything the dimmer touches is snapshotted on first use and restored exactly on exit. The dim level is saved into Capture Studio bookmarks and user looks, so a relit shot reproduces with its intended base.

On HDRP the dimmer scales scene lights only: HDRP's ambient comes from its sky and exposure system, not RenderSettings, so the ambient term is left to your volume setup there (a physically exposed HDRP scene usually responds well to dimming the sun alone).

Scripting¶

Everything the tab does is public API:

var controller = PhotoModeController.Instance;
controller.EnterPhotoMode();

// Add and edit a rig light.
var rig = controller.LightRig;
var key = rig.AddLight(PhotoModeLightKind.Key);
key.Color = Color.cyan;
key.Intensity = 3f;
rig.NotifyLightEdited(key);

// Modify a scene light under the exact-restore contract.
var snapshot = rig.TrackSceneLight(mySceneLight);
mySceneLight.intensity = 0.1f;

controller.ExitPhotoMode(); // rig destroyed, mySceneLight restored exactly

PhotoModeController.ActiveLightingCapabilities reports what the active pipeline supports (point/spot/directional/area lights, shadows, physical units) — the same honest capability gating the effects system uses, so the Light tab hides itself entirely on pipelines with no lighting backend.

Rig presets are plain ScriptableObjects (PhotoModeLightPreset): create your own via Assets → Create → Photo Mode Pro → Light Preset and assign them to the controller's Light Presets list.
