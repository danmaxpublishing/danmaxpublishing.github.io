Stickers & Frames

The Decorate tab places CC0 stickers and photo frames on the live viewfinder and bakes them into the capture at the output's full resolution — including supersampled shots and tiled 8K–32K exports, where the compositor draws into the streamed tile scanlines so a sticker straddling a tile seam stays seamless. The shipped pack holds 48 stickers in five categories (emotes, shapes & badges, speech bubbles, photo-booth props, seasonal) and 12 frames (clean borders, film & instant looks, ornate, textured).

  • Place — click a sticker in the browser to drop it at image center, already selected; drag it anywhere on the image, including partly off-edge. The layout allows up to 24 sticker instances (the counter shows n/24) plus one frame.
  • Manipulate — with a sticker selected: drag the body to move, the corner handle to scale, the top handle to rotate; the × affordance (or the Delete key) removes it; the scroll wheel scales the sticker under the cursor; Esc deselects. FLIP mirrors it, DUPLICATE (or Ctrl+D) clones it slightly offset, and SEND BACK / BRING FWD move it through the layer order (the last added renders on top).
  • Tint & opacity — a fixed swatch palette (white first = untinted) multiplies the sticker's colors, and an opacity slider fades it. The applied frame gets the same two controls; light and white art recolors cleanly, dark art stays dark by design.
  • Frames — click a frame to apply it, click it again (or NO FRAME) to clear. Frames are 9-sliced: corners never stretch and edges adapt to any output aspect and resolution, from thumbnails to 32K tiled exports and 2D scenes. The frame always renders above the stickers.
  • Gamepad — the sticks normally keep driving the camera (the AAA split); PLACE MODE hands them to the selected sticker instead: left stick moves, right stick scales (Y) and spins (X), the shoulders step-rotate 15°, Y duplicates, A confirms and B cancels. The hint bar swaps to the scheme while the mode is active.
  • Touch — drag to move; with a sticker selected, pinch scales and a two-finger twist rotates it. The handles work as a fallback, and the portrait layout keeps working.
  • Clear All — removes every sticker and the frame in one two-step confirm (same 3.5-second arm-and-confirm as every destructive control).

The decor layout is one global session layout: it auto-saves to decor.json beside your photos and reloads on the next photo-mode entry, on every platform including WebGL. Captures record the layout in the photo's metadata sidecar, so the gallery's Go To Shot restores the decoration together with the camera. Saving a look snapshots the decor too: applying a look replaces the current decoration wholesale (an empty layout is a deliberate "clean" look), while looks saved before 1.2.0 leave your current decoration untouched.

Decor bakes into the beauty image only — render-layer (AOV) exports, the clean-alpha pass and Capture Studio's watermark system are never touched by it, so mattes stay usable for compositing.

Add your own stickers and frames¶

The Decorate content is an ordinary catalog asset (Content/Decor/PhotoModeDecorCatalog.asset) referencing plain PNG textures, and the browser builds itself from whatever the catalog holds — extending it needs no UI work:

  1. Drop PNGs in the content folders — straight-alpha PNGs under Content/Decor/Stickers/<Category>/ (Emotes, ShapesAndBadges, SpeechBubbles, Props, Misc) or Content/Decor/Frames/. Stickers read best at 512 px or larger on the long side; frame sources should be at least 1024 px for clean 9-slicing.
  2. Run the builder — Tools ▸ Photo Mode Pro ▸ Build Decor Catalog rescans the folders, enforces the import settings (mipmaps, compression, non-readable, clamp, alpha-is-transparency) and rewrites the catalog. Frame 9-slice insets and display names come from Content/Decor/decor_manifest.json; a frame without a manifest entry gets a safe default inset (a quarter of its smaller dimension).
  3. Or edit the catalog directly — the asset's inspector exposes the entry list (id, display name, category, texture, frame insets, edge mode) for one-off additions. Ids must stay unique and stable: saved layouts, looks and photo sidecars reference them, and an id that disappears is skipped with a logged notice rather than an error.

A different catalog (or none) can be assigned per project on PhotoModeSettings.DecorCatalog; with no catalog assigned the Decorate tab hides entirely and captures are unaffected.
